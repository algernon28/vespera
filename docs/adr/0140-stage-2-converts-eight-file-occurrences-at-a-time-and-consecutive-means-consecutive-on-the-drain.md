# ADR-140 — Stage 2 converts eight file occurrences at a time, and "consecutive" means consecutive on the drain

- **Date**: 2026-09-22
- **Status**: accepted
- **Amends**: [ADR-071](0071-doclings-invocation-contract-is-one-sync-call-a-local-timeout-and-two-consecutive-streak-breakers.md) — in what "consecutive" means once documents are no longer processed in order, and in stating, rather than assuming, that its synchronous call shape stands under concurrency. Everything ADR-071 fixes as a number — the 5-minute call timeout, the timeout count of 3, the service-scope breaker of 5 — is carried over unchanged.
- **Keeps**: [ADR-127](0127-a-database-lock-is-waited-out-by-sqlites-busy-timeout-not-by-hikaris-connection-timeout.md)'s single-writer model, and [ADR-139](0139-a-refused-conversion-leaves-a-fault-row-and-a-step-that-completed-resolves-it-into-a-verdict.md) §3's discriminator, both of which this decision is shaped around rather than against. ADR-139 §3 is kept rather than amended: its sentence is unchanged and its rule is unchanged, and what section 5 adds is a price on the one category whose evidence this width weakens — recorded here as this decision's consequence, not as a correction to that one.
- **Settles** [#264](https://github.com/algernon28/vespera/issues/264).

## Context

Stage 2 pays a fixed, serial latency on every file occurrence it converts. Measured against the image `compose.yaml` pins — `docling-serve-cpu:v1.32.0`, reporting `docling 2.124.0` — on 2026-09-22, `POST /v1/convert/file` answers a 74-byte plain-text document in two seconds flat, against the sidecar's own reported `processing_time` of a few milliseconds. It is a hard floor at 2.000 s with almost no variance, which is what a sync endpoint polling its own job queue on a two-second tick looks like from outside.

The tick is per-request, not shared, and the sidecar drains a concurrent queue rather than serialising it. Driving the same endpoint at growing concurrency, throughput rises almost exactly 0.5 documents per second per unit of concurrency — about 0.49 doc/s serial, 1.98 at 4, 3.95 at 8, 7.86 at 16 — with every request still returning in about two seconds and no visible ceiling through 16 (24 acts the same in two runs of three; the third 24-run was an outlier at ~5.8 doc/s recorded rather than explained). The full record is [`docs/research/docling-serve-concurrency.md`](../research/docling-serve-concurrency.md).

The bound is therefore *not* the sidecar — at least not at the size the probe converted, which exercises none of the conversion work. The bound on a real corpus is the conversion behind the tick, which is the lumpy half the ticket measured on a live 129-file run: a mean of about 1.5 s per document, 39 under 10 ms against 9 over 5 s and one at 58 s. One folder this tool exists for — `A&RD_SmartPOS_BaseDocumentale` — holds **42,851 files**; at one file occurrence per tick, serially, that is roughly 24 hours of wall clock spent waiting on a poll before a single second of conversion.

Two of the four questions a concurrency change raises were already answerable from the record; two were not, and are the substance of this decision.

- [ADR-071](0071-doclings-invocation-contract-is-one-sync-call-a-local-timeout-and-two-consecutive-streak-breakers.md)'s two breakers — the consecutive-timeout count of 3 and the consecutive-service-scope breaker of 5 — are defined in terms of **serial order**: "three in a row", "five in a row", reset by a success. With N file occurrences in flight, "in a row" has no single denotation, and [ADR-139](0139-a-refused-conversion-leaves-a-fault-row-and-a-step-that-completed-resolves-it-into-a-verdict.md) §3 *depends* on it meaning something crisp: "a step that completed is a step in which five service-scope failures never landed consecutively" is the discriminator that turns a held fault into an `extraction-failed` verdict. That sentence must stay true or the completion/verdict scheme silently breaks.
- [ADR-071](0071-doclings-invocation-contract-is-one-sync-call-a-local-timeout-and-two-consecutive-streak-breakers.md)'s call-shape choice — the synchronous endpoint, refused the async submit-and-poll on the ground that it "buys a queue-depth signal nothing here needs, at the cost of a second call shape" — was written before the 2-second tick was measured, and says nothing about it. The obvious misreading of this ticket is "switch to async"; that reasoning has to be settled deliberately, because async is not the way to concurrency and may well be the wrong fix.

There is also a write-path subtlety the ticket names that this decision has to carry rather than rediscover: the heavy SQLite writes stage 2 performs — `extraction_cache`, `extraction_metric`, `shingle` — live *inside* the processor, inline with the HTTP call, not in the writer. "Keep the writer serial" is therefore not a small thing: parallelising the processor as it stands parallelises the writes too, which is exactly what [ADR-127](0127-a-database-lock-is-waited-out-by-sqlites-busy-timeout-not-by-hikaris-connection-timeout.md)'s single-writer tuning is there to avoid, with no benefit.

## Decision

### 1. Stage 2 converts concurrently, at width 8, a fixed default in code

The conversion step runs up to eight Docling calls in flight at once. The width is a **fixed default in code**, not a Profile gate, on ADR-071's own reasoning: a call timeout and a circuit-breaker streak length are "operational parameters about network and hardware capacity," not corpus judgements, and a concurrency width is the same species — it is a property of the machine the pipeline runs on, not of the corpus being judged. The width is the constant `CONVERSION_CONCURRENCY` on `ExtractionJobConfiguration`, beside `CHUNK_SIZE` and `SKIP_LIMIT` — a code default, which is what `ExtractionConcurrencyTest` pins when it reads the number back.

Eight, and not another number, because the two things that bound it pull in opposite directions and 8 is where neither is wasted. The measured fact is that the sidecar does not itself say where to stop; the real bound is the conversion work, and on that the ticket's own figures give a mean around 1.5 s with a 58 s tail. A width too high turns the win flat — the heavy tail saturates the sidecar's CPU before more file occurrences in flight help — and, more sharply, pushes the queue the sidecar holds *inside* the 5-minute per-call budget ADR-071 fixes, so a slow document now waits behind the documents ahead of it and reads as a timeout. A width too low leaves the linear win on the table. Eight captures most of that win — about 4 documents per second against 0.5 serial, an eightfold reduction on the tick wait — while a 58 s document and the queue ahead of it still sit comfortably inside five minutes, and 8 in-flight requests is a modest memory and connection cost against a JDK HTTP client.

Fixed precisely, because a test reads it and nothing else may: `static final int CONVERSION_CONCURRENCY = 8;` on `ExtractionJobConfiguration`, package-private as `CHUNK_SIZE` and `SKIP_LIMIT` beside it are, carrying a javadoc that says what the number counts, that it is a code default rather than a Profile gate and on whose reasoning, and why 8 rather than 16 or 32 — the two paragraphs above are what that javadoc has to convey, in its own words rather than by citation alone.

The width is otherwise an implementer's seam, and nothing here fixes *how* the eight calls are kept in flight. What is fixed is the number, its placement as a code default, and the three constraints sections 2, 3 and 5 put on the mechanism.

### 2. "Consecutive" means consecutive on the drain, in completion order

The two streak breakers stop meaning "in upload order" and mean "in the order their outcomes were observed on the drain." The drain is the one serial point through which every completed conversion passes — the place the metrics row is written, the verdict is decided, and the outcome is observed. The breaker and the timeout streak read that point and nothing else, and they count what they always counted: consecutive failures with no completed conversion between them.

This is more than a restatement of ADR-071, and the difference is the whole of why it is safe. Under this reading:

- **A dead sidecar still trips, and fast.** A sidecar that stops answering fails its whole in-flight wave as a block; those failures complete onto the drain consecutively, and the breaker sees five with nothing between them.
- **A flapping sidecar does not trip**, and that is correct, not a hole. Failures interleaved with successes are the case ADR-139 §3 already reads as "the sidecar answered for the neighbours" — each refusal is a property of what was uploaded, not of a sidecar that had stopped. The ticket's own feared case, "three failures interleaved with three successes across four threads," is *not* the run-stopping condition precisely because successes completed between the failures; a run that stops on it would condemn file occurrences a sidecar was still answering for. Section 5 qualifies this for one category and one only — `capacity`, where the eight calls in flight are themselves what provoked the refusal — and prices that case rather than excepting it.
- **ADR-139's discriminator reads the same, and what moves under it is evidential rather than textual.** "A step that completed is a step in which five service-scope failures never landed consecutively" holds word for word: "landed" is "completed onto the drain," and a step in which the breaker never fired is one in which they never landed five in a row. What does not hold unexamined is the *inference* [ADR-139](0139-a-refused-conversion-leaves-a-fault-row-and-a-step-that-completed-resolves-it-into-a-verdict.md) §3 draws from that sentence — *"the sidecar answered for this occurrence's neighbours, so the refusal is a property of what was uploaded."* Serially the neighbour's success was disjoint in time from the refusal and adjacent to it. At width 8 the neighbour's call overlapped the refused one, and may have been issued before it. Against a sidecar that has stopped answering that costs nothing and arguably gains: a converted document completing onto the drain *after* a refusal is a document the sidecar produced after that refusal was issued, and a sidecar that has stopped produces none. The inference fails in exactly one case, and it is a case this decision creates rather than one it inherits. Section 5 is that case, and prices it rather than excepting it.

What changes is *where* the streak lives relative to the HTTP parallelism, and that is a hazard to name rather than to leave implicit: the breaker and the timeout streak are step-scoped mutable state, and under concurrency they must be observed by the one drain thread, never mutated by the processor threads that make the Docling calls. A step-level `TaskExecutor` that lets the processor threads touch them is not faster code with a reading problem — it is a data race that quietly destroys the run-stopping guarantee. That constraint is part of this decision, not an implementation detail left to whoever builds it.

### 3. The write path stays serial; only the Docling call parallelises

ADR-127's single-writer model is kept intact, deliberately rather than by omission. Every SQLite write stage 2 performs — the conversion cache, the metric, the shingle, the verdict — stays on the serial path through which results are drained. Only the HTTP call to Docling is made concurrently.

Because the writes currently sit inside the processor beside the call, honouring this is a restructure, not a wiring change: the call and its side-effects have to be torn apart so the call can run in parallel while the writes still commit one at a time. This decision records the *shape* — serial writes, parallel conversion — and leaves the mechanism to the implementation. The step-level `TaskExecutor` shape is refused here, for the section-2 reason and for this one together: it parallelises the writes as well as the calls, breaking ADR-127, and it makes the streak beans shared mutable state, breaking section 2.

**What replaces it is stated positively, because a prohibition is something an implementer can honour and still lose the guarantee.** Stage 2's step runs on exactly one thread. Every method Spring Batch itself calls — the reader, the processor, the writer, and all four registered listeners — is invoked on the thread the step began on, and `StepBuilder.taskExecutor(...)` is never set on it. The threads this decision adds live below that line and never cross it: a worker makes the HTTP call to Docling and hands back its response, and touches no Spring Batch object, no `JdbcTemplate`, no `Ledger`, and neither streak bean. That is what makes the drain single-threaded *by construction* rather than by discipline — there is only one thread inside the step for the drain to be.

It is also why `ExtractionFaultRecorder`'s plain `ArrayList` and `ExtractionCircuitBreaker`'s plain `int` stay plain, and why making them thread-safe would be the wrong repair rather than a cheap insurance. A `CopyOnWriteArrayList` and an `AtomicInteger` would keep every fault row and still lose the run-stopping guarantee: five refusals can be delivered to a thread-safe counter in an order no observer ever saw as five in a row, and section 2's rule is about the order outcomes are observed in, not about the arithmetic. A structure that cannot lose a row is not the same thing as a sequence that cannot lose a streak, and only the second is what stops a run.

### 4. ADR-071's synchronous call shape stands, stated rather than assumed

Concurrency is orthogonal to the sync/async choice: eight concurrent synchronous calls are still one call shape, one file occurrence per call, no second endpoint. Nothing in this measurement commends the async submit-and-poll shape, and one thing argues against it — the async shape *is* the two-second tick made visible, as a `task_position` queue-depth signal the caller would then have to consume. Switching to it would not remove the latency; it would move it into the caller's sight and its polling loop. [ADR-071](0071-doclings-invocation-contract-is-one-sync-call-a-local-timeout-and-two-consecutive-streak-breakers.md)'s call shape stands, on a reason this measurement supplies rather than on the one ADR-071 recorded, which predates knowing the tick existed.

### 5. A `capacity` refusal this width provoked is the one place ADR-139's inference does not hold, and it is priced rather than excepted

[ADR-139](0139-a-refused-conversion-leaves-a-fault-row-and-a-step-that-completed-resolves-it-into-a-verdict.md) §3 reads a refusal under a completed step as a fact about the file, on the ground that the sidecar answered about the files around it. Four of the five categories that can leave a fault row carry that reading across unchanged: `target_unavailable`, `internal`, `unknown`, and a timeout ADR-071's streak has flipped out of document scope all say the same thing whether one call was in flight or eight. `capacity` does not. It is the sidecar declining work it has no room for — and how much work it is offered at once has just stopped being a property of the corpus and become this decision's own choice. A `capacity` refusal returned amid seven conversions is a refusal this width provoked, and ADR-139's inference would file it against the file that happened to be in it.

Three things bound that, and none of them is a new mechanism:

- **Eight is inside the range the probe measured without a refusal.** [`docs/research/docling-serve-concurrency.md`](../research/docling-serve-concurrency.md) §2 drove the pinned sidecar at 8, 12, 16 and 24 in flight, three runs at each level, and reports throughput as the batch size over the batch's wall clock at every one of them — figures that could not read as they do had a call come back refused rather than converted. That is read off the recorded throughput rather than measured as a claim of its own, and it is worth exactly what that makes it: evidence that 8 is not near where this sidecar starts declining work on the document size the probe used, not a promise that a heavy corpus never reaches that point.
- **The breaker still catches the case that would cost most.** A sidecar genuinely out of room refuses whole waves, and a wave's refusals complete onto the drain with nothing between them; five stop the step, and a stopped step writes no verdict for any fault under it (ADR-139 §3). What gets past that guard is refusal too sparse to make five in a row.
- **The category is on the row and in the reason.** ADR-139 made `category` the column that tells a sidecar's removal from a file's, precisely so this class of removal is answerable by a query rather than by re-converting the corpus. `capacity` is the value that query names, and the retune path is the ledger's usual one, unchanged: discard stage 2's rows for the run and run it again.

**Narrowing the discriminator to except `capacity` was considered and refused, because the narrowing has only one destination.** A fault that earns no verdict is an occurrence no stage examined, carrying no blocking verdict, still in the survivor set — which is [#265](https://github.com/algernon28/vespera/issues/265) exactly, reopened for one category by the record that closed it, and reopened in the direction that fails four stages later rather than at the refusal. An in-process retry on `capacity` is the other way out and is refused here too: ADR-071 declined retry on the service-scope categories, this ticket did not ask for it, and adding it would be a second mechanism justified by an exposure nothing has yet observed. If the consequence below is ever seen on a real corpus, that measurement is the ticket to open, and a retry bounded by the drain is what it should consider first.

## Consequences

**Stage 2's serial tick is amortised eightfold.** A run that waited one file occurrence per 2-second tick now drains up to eight at once; the 24-hour wait the ticket measured becomes roughly three hours of wall clock, before conversion work, on the same tier. The conversion itself is unchanged — it is the lumpy half, and the width of 8 does not make slow documents fast, it stops eight of them from each paying the full 2-second door.

**The timeout budget now contains a queue, and the width is the number that keeps it honest.** With up to 8 in flight, a document's effective wait includes whatever sat in front of it in the sidecar. The 5-minute per-call budget ADR-071 fixes is generous enough for a slow document even at the tail measured (58 s), and 8 is the number that keeps a pathological pile-up from reading as a timeout. This is a reason the width is a fixed default rather than an operator-set knob, and a reason 8 rather than 16 or 32.

**ADR-139 is untouched and still load-bearing.** The completion/verdict discriminator it records is preserved word for word by section 2 and qualified in its evidence, for one category, by section 5, and its fault recorder writes at the end of the step on the same serial path — nothing about concurrent conversion reaches it, because the fault that tripped the breaker is a fact about the drain, not about the HTTP call.

**ADR-127's interaction is decided and unmeasured, and the criterion asking for a measurement is declined rather than met.** The ticket asked for the interaction with ADR-127's single-writer tuning to be measured rather than assumed. It is not measured here. Saying so plainly is ADR-139 §3's own precedent — that record marked its unpinned branch *decided and unmeasured* rather than letting an argument read as evidence — and this is the same move for the same reason. What is decided instead is that the interaction is removed: section 3 keeps one writer, so the busy-timeout and pool tuning ADR-127 measured against a single-threaded pipeline governs a pipeline that is still single-threaded everywhere it writes. The measurement the criterion asks for — lock contention between concurrent writers — has no subject: it could only be taken against the concurrent-writer implementation this decision refuses to build, so taking it would mean building the thing in order to reject it. What a measurement would still buy, if it is ever wanted, is the *changed* single-writer profile rather than a contended one: section 3 shortens each chunk's write transaction by whatever the conversions inside it took, where today that transaction spans them. That moves contention in the direction ADR-127 wants, which is why nothing here rests on it.

**The breaker's hazard is closed by construction and pinned in two places, neither of them a type.** Section 3 puts one thread inside the step, so the streak beans have no second observer to race with. Nothing pins that by type, and nothing should: the property is about how the step is wired, not about either class, and a thread-safe counter would satisfy a type-level pin while still losing the streak. It is pinned where it is observable instead, and the two halves are different claims. `ExtractionConcurrencyTest` drives the shipped `ExtractionCircuitBreaker` from a drain of its own, fed by conversions genuinely running together at this width, and claims both halves of section 2 — what stops a run and what does not — while claiming that every call into the breaker arrived from the one draining thread and from none of the converting ones. `ExtractionStepTest` claims the half no unit can: that the shipped step really keeps this many conversions in flight at once, and really converts on threads other than the one it was invoked on. A step-level `TaskExecutor` passes the first and is the thing the second exists to catch.

**The research record is generated, not transcribed.** [`docs/research/docling-serve-concurrency.md`](../research/docling-serve-concurrency.md) carries the round-trip and concurrency measurements this decision rests on, produced by driving the pinned sidecar on 2026-09-22, and states what it did not measure — the real-corpus conversion distribution, which stays the ticket's own live-run figures.

**A sidecar declining work this width offered it can cost archive, and the exposure ADR-139 booked grows with the width.** ADR-139 recorded the case — a run that completes with fewer than five consecutive service-scope failures in it, some of them genuinely the sidecar's, a capacity blip between two good documents — and bounded it by the breaker. Both halves move here. A blip of a given length is now offered eight times as many calls as it was serially, so it can refuse eight times as many occurrences; and the conversions from the same wave complete onto the drain between those refusals, which is exactly what keeps five of them from landing in a row, so the breaker's bound bites later than it did. The shape to expect is a wave that comes back part refused and part converted, costing several occurrences their archive where a serial run would either have lost one or have tripped the breaker and lost none. It is accepted rather than merely noticed, on section 5's three bounds and on what the alternative costs: the serial pipeline that avoids it spends a measured twenty-four hours of tick on one of this corpus's four folders, and loses an entire invocation to the very case [#265](https://github.com/algernon28/vespera/issues/265) reported.

**Nothing here changes the seed side**, ADR-083's `unusable_seed` path, or stage 6's exemplar-first call — each of those conversions is its own question, and this decision is stage 2's.
